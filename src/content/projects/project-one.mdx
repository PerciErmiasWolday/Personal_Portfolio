## Problem

My cousin could read the words on a page, but sometimes the difficult part was understanding what wasn't directly stated: why someone was upset, what sarcasm meant, or who a pronoun referred to.

I wanted to help with those moments without turning reading into a series of interruptions or comprehension tests.

## Approach

I started with one rule: reading comes first, and help appears only when requested.

Readers can select a sentence to see a short explanation and the exact part of the story supporting it. I also added optional help markers, personal reading uploads, and comprehension questions.

I didn't want the AI to invent explanations that sounded convincing. So I built checks that verify whether the quoted evidence actually exists in the passage, with a fallback when the response can't be validated.

## Architecture

I built the reading interface in React and TypeScript, with a Python/FastAPI backend handling text processing and AI requests.

When a reader opens a story, the backend divides it into sections and assigns sentence IDs. When the reader requests help, the application sends the relevant context to the OpenAI API. The response goes through validation checks before the explanation and its supporting quote appear beside the selected sentence.

Saved readings and progress stay in the browser.

## Results

I tested ReadingBridge using 30 hand-written evaluation cases covering sarcasm, implied feelings, pronouns, idioms, cause and effect, and missing information.

- **30/30**: Passed first-pass evidence-grounding checks
- **25/30**: Correctly determined whether the text provided enough information to answer
- **11/24**: Selected the expected best supporting clue in answerable cases

The results revealed an important limitation. The model could quote something from the passage and still choose the wrong evidence. It also sometimes guessed who an ambiguous pronoun referred to when the story didn't establish an answer.

These results gave me a clearer direction for improving evidence selection and handling uncertainty.

## What I learned

The hardest part wasn't getting AI to explain a sentence. It was figuring out when that explanation could actually be trusted.

I learned that making a model cite evidence is only the first step. The evidence has to be relevant, and sometimes the correct answer is simply that the story doesn't give us enough information.

That's the part I'm most interested in improving next.
