## Problem

After a customer call, someone still has to go through the recording, write down what matters, update customer records, and remember what needs to happen next.

I wanted to see how much of that process I could automate, while keeping the information organized and accessible when someone needed to revisit a conversation.

## Approach

I started with transcription and summarization, then realized that a summary alone wasn't enough. The useful information needed somewhere to go.

I expanded the application into a CRM system that extracts customer names, call outcomes, key details, and follow-up tasks. I also added search and analytics so users could revisit conversations and identify information across different calls.

One of my main design decisions was to connect these features to the same backend rather than building separate pages with disconnected data.

## Architecture

I built the frontend using React, Vite, and Tailwind CSS, with a Python/FastAPI backend handling the processing.

When a user uploads a recording, the backend uses Whisper to transcribe the audio. The transcript is then summarized, and AI-assisted extraction identifies structured information such as customer details, call outcomes, and next steps.

I used SQLAlchemy and SQLite to store processed meetings and their associated CRM records. The dashboard, Calls, CRM, Ask AI, and Analytics views retrieve information through the backend API, allowing the application to work with the same underlying records.

The Ask AI feature also allows users to search saved customer information using natural-language questions.

## Results

I tested the complete recording-upload workflow, from submitting an audio file to seeing the processed call appear in the application.

The recording was successfully transcribed, summarized, and saved. The new call appeared in the Calls page, with the transcript available for review.

I also verified that the upload interface and navigation between the Calls and CRM pages worked correctly.

**Verified outcomes**

- End-to-end recording upload and processing
- Searchable transcripts and customer records
- Connected dashboard, CRM, and analytics views

This was a functional prototype test, not a measured improvement in staff productivity or customer service.

## What I learned

One challenge I hadn't fully anticipated was that a transcription mistake could carry over into the customer record. A name that sounded close enough in a transcript could still be completely wrong for a CRM.

I also learned that getting the AI output wasn't the end of the problem. I had to think about what happened when extraction failed, whether records were saved correctly, and how different parts of the application stayed consistent.

It made me pay much more attention to data reliability, not just whether an AI feature appeared to work.
