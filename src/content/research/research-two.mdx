## Abstract

I worked on a research project exploring whether neural networks could use classical physics to predict quantum behavior. Using Python, I developed simulations of classical and quantum systems, generated paired trajectories, and trained neural networks to learn the relationship between them. I started with harmonic oscillators before moving to fluxonium, a superconducting circuit used in quantum computing. The goal was to understand whether machine learning could approximate complex quantum dynamics using simpler classical information.

## Research question

**Can a neural network predict quantum behavior from classical motion without repeatedly running expensive quantum simulations?**

Classical and quantum systems describe physical behavior differently. I wanted to investigate whether their trajectories contain enough shared information for a neural network to learn a useful connection.

## Method

I used NumPy and SciPy to simulate classical dynamics and QuTiP and scqubits for quantum simulations. For the fluxonium experiments, I generated **200 paired trajectories, each containing 100 time points**, using corresponding initial conditions.

I trained feed-forward neural networks in PyTorch using Adam optimization and mean squared error, then evaluated their predictions against simulated quantum expectation values.

## Findings

The experiments showed how challenging it is to learn a reliable classical-to-quantum mapping. Although the networks produced predictions, comparing their performance against simple baselines revealed that low prediction error alone doesn't establish that a model has learned the underlying physics.

These results helped identify limitations in the initial approach and directions for improving the models and evaluation.

## Why it matters

Quantum simulations can be computationally expensive, especially for complex systems. Learning useful connections between classical and quantum dynamics could eventually make certain simulations more accessible.

What I found most valuable was learning to question what a neural network has actually learned, rather than judging its success by prediction accuracy alone.
