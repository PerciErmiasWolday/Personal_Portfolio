## Abstract

I worked with Professor Andrew Thall at Alma College to investigate how autonomous systems can plan efficient, smooth paths when their surroundings are uncertain or constantly changing. My research combined motion prediction, Kalman filtering, and model predictive control to evaluate how different planning strategies respond to uncertainty. Through simulations and performance comparisons, I explored how robots can adapt their movements without sacrificing efficiency or stability.

## Research question

**How can an autonomous system adjust its path in unpredictable environments while keeping its movements smooth and efficient?**

A robot might calculate an ideal path, but that plan can quickly become outdated when obstacles move or sensor measurements are imperfect. I wanted to understand how often a system should replan and which strategies balance responsiveness with stable movement.

## Method

I developed and evaluated motion-planning simulations using Kalman filtering to estimate system states and MPC to guide movement. I experimented with replanning strategies and planning heuristics under changing conditions, comparing their effects on path efficiency and acceleration variance.

I also built visualizations to examine robot trajectories and better understand how different decisions affected movement.

## Findings

The evaluated approach achieved **93% path efficiency** while reducing **acceleration variance by 28%** in our experiments. These results demonstrated that the planning strategy could maintain efficient trajectories while producing smoother movement under the simulated conditions.

I presented the research findings through a scientific poster.

## Why it matters

Autonomous vehicles, delivery robots, and other robotic systems must make decisions using imperfect information. A path isn't useful if a robot can't safely adapt when its environment changes.

This project showed me how much the quality of a planning algorithm depends on balancing competing goals: reaching a destination efficiently, responding to uncertainty, and maintaining stable motion.
